A provably optimal low-storage Runge–Kutta method

Large-scale simulations in computational fluid dynamics rely on low-storage Runge–Kutta methods. We gave Object Run a method published this year and asked it to lower the error without adding any cost in computation, memory or stability. After 1 hour 37 minutes it returned a new method with 10.65% lower leading error, together with a proof that no method under these constraints can do better.

Background

When an explicit Runge–Kutta method solves y′ = f(t, y), each step evaluates the right-hand side several times and stores the intermediate results. Williamson's 2N format needs only two storage vectors regardless of the number of stages, which makes it popular in large simulations limited by GPU or main memory. The price is that the coefficients must satisfy extra algebraic relations, so the design space is smaller than for general methods.

Bazavov's paper in BIT Numerical Mathematics systematically derives the order conditions for the 2N format and gives several examples with rational coefficients. We took the five-stage, third-order method from its Table 8 as the reference.

Setup

We fixed everything that affects cost and stability: 5 function evaluations per step, third-order accuracy, the original 2N recurrence, and a stability polynomial identical to the reference method's. Under these constraints only 10 low-storage coefficients remain free. The objective is the 2-norm of the fourth-order error coefficient vector as defined by NodePy, i.e. the leading truncation error coefficients of a third-order method. Results were judged by a verifier fixed in advance: it rebuilds the Butcher tableau from the submitted coefficients and checks every constraint at 100, 160 and 180 decimal digits of precision.

There was also an unexpected finding: while reconstructing the reference method exactly, we found that the a51 = 12053/11172 printed in the paper's Table 8 is a typo; the correct value is 2053/11172.

Results

The leading error norm dropped from 0.015632 to 0.013967. More importantly, the model found why it cannot go lower: of the four error components, one depends only on the z4 coefficient of the stability polynomial, which is fixed at −639/45752 in this setting. The new method makes the other three components exactly zero, so 639/45752 is the theoretical lower bound under these constraints, and the new method attains it exactly. Under these constraints, the new method is the optimum.

To rule out numerical error, we used exact rational interval arithmetic to build the system of equations and its Jacobian independently from the original recurrence, and used the interval Newton method to prove that a unique algebraic solution exists inside a tiny rational box. The stored coefficients are within 3.5×10−180 of that solution. The proof does not rely on any polynomial, Jacobian or verdict produced by the model.

On four nonlinear ODEs that never took part in optimization, the new method lowers the error by 24% to 41% on three of them at the same computational cost.

Notes

Optimality holds for the fixed five-stage 2N structure and the fixed stability polynomial; methods with a different stability polynomial belong to a different feasible set. The new method's largest coefficient in absolute value is 5.32 (1.49 for the original). Error on the logistic test problem rises by about 8.5%. The run made 88 model requests and cost US$1.60 in API spend.
