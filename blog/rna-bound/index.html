Proving the design limit of an RNA structure

Finding a good RNA sequence only shows what is possible. Proving that no sequence can do better shows where the limit of design lies. For Repetitious Sequences 8/10 in Eterna100, Object Run enumerated 8.15 billion base assignments and tightened the provable upper bound on the folding probability from 0.1874 to 0.1577.

Background

In a thermodynamic model, an RNA sequence can fold into many secondary structures, and the probability of each is determined by its free energy. A designer wants the target structure's probability to be as high as possible, but some structures have inherent competitors: whatever sequence is chosen, other structures always take a sizable share of the probability.

arXiv:2602.13610 proposes a method that gives upper bounds of this kind. It decomposes the target structure into structural motifs, picks a set of competing structures for each motif, computes an upper limit on the target structure's probability over every base assignment of the motif that satisfies the pairing constraints, and then combines the per-motif results into an overall bound. The better the competing structures, the tighter the bound.

Setup

The target structure and energy model were fixed before the run. The main target was Repetitious Sequences 8/10, whose upper bound reproduced from the authors' public cache is 0.18740126; the fallback target was Chicken feet. The energy model is ViennaRNA 2.7.0 with Turner 2004 parameters at 37°C, without pseudoknots. The model's job was to find a better combination of competing structures; whether a bound holds was decided by an independent verifier.

Results

The model used 14 competing structures on the core motif. That motif has 5 paired sites and 10 unpaired sites, for 65 × 410 = 8,153,726,976 feasible assignments, all of which the verifier enumerated. The three short hairpin motifs each used 15 competing structures and cover 147,456 assignments each. The combined overall bound is about 0.157700067, 15.85% tighter than the authors' result.

Verification

The verifier uses none of the numbers reported by the model. It rederives the variables from the target structure, rebuilds the energy parameter tables, checks that every competing structure is a legal secondary structure and that the motif decomposition preserves the boundary conditions, then fully enumerates every assignment and wraps the floating-point results in conservative interval envelopes. Maxima from random sampling and unfinished enumerations are never accepted as bounds. The final bound is given to 60 decimal places, with every rounding in the direction that works against the claim.

Notes

Results are based on ViennaRNA 2.7.0 with the Turner 2004 energy model. The fallback target from the same run, Chicken feet, did not improve. The run made 337 model requests, cost US$1.02 in API spend and took 2 hours 7 minutes.
